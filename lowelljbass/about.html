About

Twenty-five years of playing, and about as long teaching it.

Add a portrait — instrument in hand

I started on upright bass in a jazz combo at seventeen, drawn in by how much the bass player got to decide without ever taking the spotlight. Twenty-five years on, that's still the appeal.

The genres shifted long before the approach did. Jazz clubs gave way to function bands, then session work, and for the last decade or so the bulk of my playing has been contemporary rock, pop and worship music — different vocabulary, same job: listen harder than everyone else on stage, and make the song easier to feel.

Teaching came out of necessity as much as enjoyment. Players kept asking how I got a part to sit right, and explaining it turned out to sharpen my own playing as much as theirs. I've been teaching bass — and guitar, as a secondary focus for students who want both — for most of my playing career.

The journey

Twenty-five years, four chapters

Early years

Learning the language in jazz clubs

Started on upright and electric in small jazz combos — learning to walk a bassline, comp behind a soloist, and listen before playing.

Building the craft

Function bands and session work

Years on the covers and function-band circuit, plus session calls for regional artists — the fastest way to learn how many different pockets a song can need.

Teaching takes root

First students, first studio

Began teaching bass alongside playing, later adding guitar for students who wanted one teacher across both instruments.

Today

Worship, rock and pop stages

Most weeks now split between a worship team, rock and pop session work, and a full lesson roster — with courses built for the students I can't reach every week.

How I teach

Technique in service of the song

I don't teach scales for their own sake. Every technical idea gets tied back to a real part you'll actually play — a walk-up into a chorus, a muted eighth-note groove, a fill that doesn't step on the vocal. Ear training happens from week one, because reading a chart will only get you so far on a bandstand.

Guitar lessons follow the same approach for students who play both instruments, with an emphasis on rhythm playing and how the two instruments lock together in a mix.
